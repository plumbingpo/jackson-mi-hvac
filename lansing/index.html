When Your System Fails in Lansing

If your heat or air quits, how quickly help arrives matters more than anything else. For that reason, we keep our schedule flexible across the area and answer every call with a live person. Urgent calls usually get a technician within a day, and we treat no-heat and no-cool calls ahead of routine work.

The urgent calls we answer usually follow a pattern. On the cooling side, it's warm air from the vents, an outdoor unit that hums without starting, or an evaporator coil frozen solid. On the heating side, it's a furnace that lights and then shuts down. And any time of year, it's breakers that trip every cycle, clogged condensate drains overflowing into the furnace cabinet, and thermostats that have quit. Since our trucks carry capacitors, contactors, igniters, flame sensors, universal control boards, and fittings, most of these are wrapped up in one visit, with the price agreed before any work begins.

Two situations call for quick action from you before a technician arrives. The first is an iced-over air conditioner: turn the system off at the thermostat and set the fan to "on" so the moving air thaws the coil, because running it frozen can overflow the drain pan and damage the compressor. The second is a furnace that short-cycles or produces a burning smell. Turn it off at the service switch and call us. Those few minutes can save you from a much larger repair.

Getting More Years From Your HVAC System in Lansing

Most mid-season breakdowns trace back to maintenance that never happened, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Swap your filter every one to three months, clear vegetation at least two feet from the outdoor condenser, and rinse the condenser coil every spring. Then book a professional tune-up twice a year, one for cooling and one for heating, so our technicians can catch small problems while they're still cheap to fix.

The age of your equipment matters just as much. With regular service, central air usually runs 12 to 15 years, a furnace 15 to 20, and a heat pump 10 to 15. Once a system passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like rising energy bills, rooms that won't reach the setpoint, or one repair after another, it's time to plan a replacement. A planned replacement costs less and causes far less stress than scrambling for a new system during the worst heat wave of the year.

Minor issues rarely stay small when they're ignored. Take a weak capacitor: replacing it is a short, inexpensive repair, but left alone for a season, it can burn out the compressor, and suddenly a minor fix becomes a full system replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to save our number in their phone and reach out at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves money and spares you a few miserable nights.
